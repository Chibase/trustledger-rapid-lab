# Stakeholder Risk Intelligence Framework

## Core Intelligence Chain

The research foundation follows this conceptual chain:

```
Stakeholder Conditions
→ Stakeholder Signals
→ Sentiment
→ Behaviour
→ Events / Incidents
→ Observed Outcomes
→ Patterns
→ Risk Indicators
→ Likelihood
→ Consequence
→ Prediction
→ Validation
```

## Two-Tiered Architecture

### Core Stakeholder Conditions (Primary Assessment)
These are the 8 core stakeholder conditions that form the foundation of the risk assessment:

1. **Stakeholder Coverage** - The extent to which relevant stakeholders are identified and understood
2. **Influence & Power** - The distribution of influence and power among stakeholder groups
3. **Expectations & Perceptions** - Stakeholder expectations and perceptions of the project
4. **Engagement & Relationships** - Quality of engagement and relationship management
5. **Grievance & Conflict** - Existence and management of grievances and conflicts
6. **Benefits / Employment / Empowerment** - Distribution of benefits, employment, and empowerment opportunities
7. **Institutional & Governance** - Institutional alignment and governance structures
8. **Change / Disruption / External** - Management of change, disruption, and external factors

### Contextual Risk Lenses (Secondary Assessment)
These risk lenses provide the context through which stakeholder conditions are evaluated:

1. **Financial** - Financial implications and risks
2. **Operational** - Operational impacts and risks
3. **Regulatory** - Regulatory compliance and risks
4. **Environmental** - Environmental impacts and risks
5. **Social** - Social implications and risks
6. **Political** - Political considerations and risks
7. **Reputational** - Reputational impacts and risks
8. **Market** - Market-related risks and implications
9. **Institutional** - Institutional factors and risks
10. **Change** - Change-related risks and implications

## Assessment Structure

### Primary Assessment: Core Stakeholder Conditions
- 8 conditions (Core Stakeholder Conditions)
- 5 questions per condition = 40 total questions
- Each question assesses the condition through specific risk lenses
- Scoring focuses on the stakeholder condition severity

### Secondary Assessment: Contextual Risk Lenses
- Risk lenses are embedded within questions as contextual factors
- Each question maps to one or more risk lenses
- Risk lens scores are calculated from individual question responses (not condition scores)
- Provides risk context for each stakeholder condition

## Mathematical Transformation

### Condition Strength
```
Condition Strength = Average of applicable question responses (0-100%)

Where each question response is converted from 0-4 scale to 0-100%:
Response 0 = 0%
Response 1 = 25%
Response 2 = 50%
Response 3 = 75%
Response 4 = 100%

N/A responses are excluded from the denominator.
```

### Risk Exposure
```
Risk Exposure (per lens) = Average of applicable question responses mapped to that lens (0-100%)

Risk lens scores are calculated from INDIVIDUAL question responses, NOT from condition scores.

For each risk lens:
1. Identify all questions mapped to that lens
2. Convert each applicable response to percentage (0-100%)
3. Calculate average of applicable responses
4. This becomes the risk exposure score for that lens

Overall Risk Exposure = Average of all risk lens scores
```

### Key Methodology Correction
**Previous approach (incorrect):** Applied entire condition score to every question/lens
**Current approach (correct):** Risk lens scores calculated from individual question responses mapped to that lens

This separation ensures:
- **Condition Strength** reflects the health/strength of the underlying stakeholder condition
- **Risk Exposure** reflects potential exposure associated with weakness in that condition/pathway
- Risk lens scores are based on actual evidence (individual responses) rather than aggregated condition scores

## Evidence Classification

Every P02 intelligence output is classified as one of:

### Observed
Directly recorded evidence (e.g., actual grievance counts, meeting attendance)

### Calculated
Mathematically derived from recorded evidence (e.g., condition scores, risk lens scores)

### Inferred
Interpretation based on available evidence (e.g., risk bands, priority levels)

### Predicted
An estimate of a future/unknown outcome generated by a defined predictive model (not currently implemented)

## Research Integrity

The prototype does NOT claim:
- Scientifically validated event probabilities
- Reliable monetary-loss prediction
- Verified community sentiment
- Verified stakeholder behaviour prediction
- Predictive validity

Instead, P02 establishes the architecture required to investigate these capabilities.

## Research Progression

```
Assessment
→ Observation
→ Pattern
→ Outcome relationship
→ Prediction
→ Validation
```

## Future Capabilities

The prototype establishes the foundation for researching:

1. Probability of a defined stakeholder-related event
2. Potential project/financial consequence of that event
3. Actual stakeholder/community sentiment
4. Actual stakeholder behaviour

These require validation through the prediction/outcome validation loop.
